Kitchen Water Damage: What Glendale Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. Our Glendale crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

In a wet kitchen, the cabinets are where the real expense lies. Boxes made of particleboard swell quickly and do not shrink back, while plywood and solid wood often dry out well. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

What It Looks Like

  • Sticking cabinet doors, or doors whose edges no longer match up
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Swelling and flaking where the cabinet sides meet the floor
  • Soft or discolored vinyl flooring near the refrigerator or along the cabinet line
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Toe Kick Space

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water pools in it for weeks, and the mold that grows goes unseen until the cabinets come out.

Layers of Flooring

Many kitchens have a newer floor installed on top of an older one. Trapped water sits between the two, with no way to evaporate and no sign on the surface.

The Ice Maker Line

An ice maker is fed by a thin tube that may lose a few drops each minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Behind the dishwasher and sink, the wall carries both the drain and the supply lines. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

The Damage Timeline

First hour

Water Runs Under

When a hose or fitting fails, water heads behind the cabinets and beneath the appliances. Seams and edges let it get below the flooring.

24 to 48 hours

Cabinets Begin to Swell

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. In the dark, enclosed gap below the cabinets, and at the back of the dishwasher, mold can start.

Days 3 through 7

Flooring Starts to Move

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.

What We Do

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. We confirm dry readings, then reinstall appliances and help you line up any repairs that remain.
