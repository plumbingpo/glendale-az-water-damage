Do This First

Before We Arrive

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. If it is stuck or leaking, shut off the main water valve for the house.
  3. Watch for Hot WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Let pooled water cool off before anyone steps in it, and keep kids and pets out of the area.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Coverage

Insurance and Water Heater Leak Cleanup

A sudden tank failure is often treated as accidental and sudden, so the resulting damage to belongings, floors and walls is often covered. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. Because coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks, a claim may be denied or reduced when the adjuster sees indications the tank leaked for a long time, like old stains or trails of rust. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

Why It Happens

How It Usually Starts

  • Pressure rising too high in the tank because the thermostat failed or no expansion tank was installed.
  • The tank rusting from within after its anode rod has worn away.
  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • The relief valve for temperature and pressure opening, or failing, and letting water out.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Platform and Subfloor

Under the tank, the wood stand or the subfloor absorbs the largest share of the leak. Rot can weaken it, a real concern once a full new tank sits on top.

Heater Closet Drywall

When the heater closet is indoors, its drywall draws water upward and passes it to rooms on the far side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

Stored Belongings

Whatever is kept by a closet or garage heater, from keepsakes to furniture and boxes, is the first thing to get wet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Ceilings Under an Attic Heater

Water from an attic unit runs down through insulation to the ceiling beneath it. Soaked insulation loses its insulating value and gets heavy enough to collapse the drywall ceiling.
