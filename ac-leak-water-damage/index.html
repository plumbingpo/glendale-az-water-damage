AC Leak Water Damage Help in Glendale

Cooling a house also dries the air, which means the unit is constantly making water it must get rid of. In a healthy system the coil sheds it into a pan, and a narrow drain line carries it away. Algae and dust can plug the line, and then the pan fills up and overflows. If the air handler sits in a hallway closet or the attic, that water ends up in the nearby floor, wall or ceiling.

A slow drip causes more harm than most homeowners would guess. It soaks the stand the unit sits on, climbs into the drywall and spreads beneath the flooring, and by the time you see a mark mold has frequently begun. We find how far the moisture spread, dry the structure and repair what was damaged. Clearing the drain or repairing the unit remains a job for an HVAC technician, and our schedule can fit around theirs.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Shut the System DownSwitch the system off at the thermostat so it stops making condensate. If water is near the air handler's wiring, turn off its breaker as well.
  2. Avoid Wet Wiring and LightsDo not touch the unit, nearby outlets or ceiling lights that have water in or around them until power to that area is off.
  3. Empty the Drain PanUse a wet-vac or rags to take the standing water out of the pan and stop the spill, provided the pan is safe to reach. Crawling over a wet attic to get there is not worth it.
  4. Soak Up What You CanMove clothes, boxes and furniture back from the stained area or the closet, catch ceiling drips in a bucket, wipe up hard floors and blot the carpet.
  5. Bring In Two ProsAn HVAC technician should clear the drain line, and our team should find out where the water went. Until that repair is done, the unit stays off.
Coverage

Insurance and AC Leak Water Damage

Coverage for an AC leak usually comes down to timing. A sudden pan overflow that you dealt with promptly is the kind of loss homeowners policies often cover. If the adjuster decides the drip went on for weeks or months, it may be treated as a gradual leak or a maintenance issue and denied. Repairing the air conditioner itself is typically not covered either way. Our photos and readings are available for your claim, and your policy has the final word.

How We Fix It

How We Handle AC Leak Water Damage

We use moisture meters and thermal imaging around the air handler, the ceiling or walls next to it, and the flooring outside the closet to find every wet material, not only the stained ones.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A condensate pump that burned out or lost power and stopped moving water.
  • A drain line joint that came apart or was never glued, leaking inside a wall or attic.
  • No working float switch to stop the system when the pan filled.
  • The drain pipe for condensate blocked by a buildup of dust and algae.
  • Sweating ducts and pipes in a humid attic wetting the drywall under them.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Closet Walls and Platform

Closet units often sit on a wood platform over the return air box. Water rots that platform and wicks up the surrounding drywall, where it stays hidden behind the unit.

Baseboards and Door Trim

The bottom of the door jamb and the baseboard beside it take on water early. Because pressed-wood trim puffs up and falls apart when wet, new material goes in.

Mold Around the Air Handler

Within 24 to 48 hours mold can start on wet wood and drywall next to the unit, and the return may draw spores into the ducts and around the home.

Drywall Below the Air Handler

An attic air handler leaks onto the drywall beneath it. Staining is the early sign, sagging follows, and drywall left wet for days can come down into the room below.
